# Austria

Reviewed on 25 September 2026, mainly from official sources in German. This is the second country review, after the [United Kingdom](united-kingdom.md). It is a desk review: each topic from [the codebook](../codebook/codebook.md) was looked for in federal and provincial legislation, Interior Ministry pages, and the binding rules of the Österreichische Fußball-Bundesliga (ÖFBL) and the Österreichischer Fußball-Bund (ÖFB). Recommendation Rec(2022)2 was also searched for by name.

Austria is a State Party to the Saint-Denis Convention. It signed on 22 February 2017, ratified on 3 September 2021, and the Convention entered into force for Austria on 1 October 2021. That is before Rec(2022)2 was adopted. No source checked in this review cites Recommendation Rec(2022)2 by name. The Convention text itself is published in the Austrian Legal Information System as BGBl. III Nr. 124/2021.

## The short answer

Austria already requires, and has required since long before 2022, the core of Annex 1 for larger sports events: a provincial venue permit (Veranstaltungsstättenbewilligung or equivalent), organiser duties for an Ordnungsdienst, police powers around Sportgroßveranstaltungen, and nationwide football stadium bans under ÖFB rules, with a national football information point in the Interior Ministry.

It has not adopted the recommendation as a single national law or a single federal stewarding regulation. Safety and event licensing sit mainly in the nine Länder. Policing and sports-event police powers sit in the federal Sicherheitspolizeigesetz. Spectator service and detailed stewarding for top-division football sit in binding Bundesliga and ÖFB instruments. Where this review could read the position for the highest football division and for selected Land laws (Vienna, Styria, Upper Austria), and not for every Land, that is stated.

Stewarding is stronger for Bundesliga matches than the UK pilot found for the United Kingdom as a whole. The Ordnerrichtlinien for the highest division require training, a recognisable steward identity, search and expulsion powers grounded in Hausrecht, and a chain of command under the club Sicherheitsverantwortlicher. That is still not a federal Annex 2 regulation for all sports events.

## What has changed since 1 September 2022

These are the changes found. The provincial venue-permit regime and the SPG sports-event powers themselves were already in place.

- **27–29 September 2022.** The Interior Ministry held a Forum “Sicherheit im Sport” and presented a platform described as “Sicherheit und Sport” — a living document on police challenges at sports events, reported as updated since 2016. This review did not open the full platform text.
- **1 July 2024.** The Bundesliga Sicherheitsrichtlinien for the highest division took effect in the version read here.
- **22–23 July 2024.** BGBl. I Nr. 122/2024 amended the Sicherheitspolizeigesetz. Section 41 on special search orders at large events was widened to cover particularly vulnerable facilities and installations, not only events. Refusal of a search can lead to exclusion from entry.
- **1 July 2025.** The Bundesliga Stadionbestimmungen version read here took effect (minimum capacity, segregation, CCTV, evacuation plan with proof of tests and training, wheelchair places).
- **1 January 2025.** The consolidated Gewerbeordnung text for the Bewachungsgewerbe (including Ordner- und Kontrolldienste bei Veranstaltungen) is marked in force from that date in the commercial-law database used here; this review did not re-read the amending Act line by line.
- **1 July 2026.** The Bundesliga Ordnerrichtlinien for the 2026/27 season took effect. Training every two years for fan-sector and pitch-side stewards, with a Bundesliga Ordnerausweis, remains binding.
- **1 July 2026.** Wiener Veranstaltungsgesetz 2020 § 31 (Sicherheitskonzept) has a new version in force from that date (LGBl. Nr. 33/2025). The previous text, still visible as the version until 30 June 2026, already required a Sicherheitskonzept for events over 5,000 spectators, including Ordnungsdienst and evacuation plans with attention to disabled persons. The detailed delta of the July 2026 text was not re-read section by section.
- **2025.** The sports ministry’s EU annual preview notes cooperation with the Council of Europe on the “Balance S4” project to strengthen the security and service pillars of the Saint-Denis Convention. That is project cooperation, not a new statute.

## Federal, Land, and association rules — not one statute

**Federal policing.** The Sicherheitspolizeigesetz gives the security authorities power to declare a Sicherheitsbereich around a Sportgroßveranstaltung (§ 49a), to ban a person from that area for the duration of the ordinance (§ 49a Abs. 2), to summon persons for a Gefährderansprache (§ 49b), and to impose a Meldeauflage requiring personal appearance at a police station around a designated event (§ 49c). Secondary commentary and training materials describe a Gewalttäterdatei Sport and data sharing toward stadium bans. The full database paragraphs were not re-opened in RIS in this pass; confidence on the database line is therefore medium.

**National football information point.** Referat II/BPD/5/b in the Interior Ministry houses the Nationale Fußballinformationsstelle (NFIS), expressly tied to Council Decision 2002/348/JI. Its published tasks include concepts for public safety at sports events, guidelines for the szenekundiger Dienst, an international fan database, and coordination of preventive fan support. The page was last updated 19 August 2026.

**Land event and venue law.** Venue safety licensing is provincial. Upper Austria’s Veranstaltungssicherheitsgesetz provides for a Veranstaltungsstättenbewilligung that can set capacity and conditions; a 2015 administrative court case on a football stadium confirms that model in practice. Styria’s Veranstaltungsgesetz 2012 § 5 requires an Ordnerdienst, fire and medical cover where violence or rival supporter groups are expected, and for high-risk stadium events also segregation, controlled ticketing, refusal of entry for intoxicated persons and those with prohibited items, and promotion of house rules. Vienna’s Veranstaltungsgesetz 2020 § 31 requires a Sicherheitskonzept for events above 5,000 spectators (and for higher-risk events below that threshold), covering Ordnungsdienst, access control, counting systems, communication with emergency services, and alarm/evacuation plans including disabled persons. Other Länder were not opened in full.

**Association rules for top football.** For the highest Bundesliga division, the Sicherheitsrichtlinien, Ordnerrichtlinien, and Stadionbestimmungen are binding instructions under disciplinary power. They require a Sicherheitsverantwortlicher, a Fanbeauftragter, placard/house rules, segregation, prohibited items, searches, a Krisenkontaktgruppe with police, CCTV, an approved evacuation plan with proof of tests, and trained Ordner. The ÖFB Stadionverbotsordnung (in force 1 July 2021) provides nationwide private-law stadium bans administered by the Prävention & Stadionverbote committee, with a shared ban list for clubs, associations, and the Interior Ministry.

## Topic by topic

Each topic is one cluster from the codebook. The word in bold is the judgement for Austria as a whole. Detail that differs by Land or that rests only on Bundesliga rules is in the paragraph.

### National governance and coordination — partial

The Interior Ministry’s NFIS and sports-policing structures coordinate the police pillar nationally. Venue licensing and much of the organiser safety duty sit with the Länder. The Bundesliga and ÖFB set detailed duties for professional football. No single national body was found whose statutory job is safety, security, and service together in the sense of Annex 1’s national committee.

Local multi-agency coordination is required in practice for risk matches: Land laws require Ordnungsdienst and safety concepts; Bundesliga rules require a match-day Sicherheitsrundgang and a Krisenkontaktgruppe with police, fire, medical, and steward command. That is met for top-division football and for the Land instruments read.

A published national strategy covering safety, security, and service and kept under review was not opened as a full official text. The Convention is in force. The BMI platform “Sicherheit und Sport” was announced in September 2022 as a living document; this review did not read it. Status: not found as a confirmed published strategy, with a survey question.

Roles of public authorities, organiser, and venue are stated across the SPG, Land event laws, and association rules. That is met in substance for football events, though split across instruments.

### Venue safety duties — met for the operator and regulations at top football; partial for a national integrated duty

Land laws place safety and orderly running on the Veranstalter, including Ordnerdienst and refusal of entry. Bundesliga rules make the home club responsible before, during, and after the match, and require a Sicherheitsverantwortlicher (safety officer equivalent) with listed tasks, including the evacuation plan and liaison with the security authority. House/Platzordnung is required and must be displayed; it is a condition of entry in practice and under association rules.

An integrated safety, security and service approach is the language of the Convention, which is in force for Austria, and of BMI communications about Saint-Denis. It is not read here as a single domestic statute that invents that duty for every sports event. Partial.

### Safety certification and licensing — met

A public venue permit or equivalent building/event approval is required before spectators are admitted to designated venues. Upper Austria’s Veranstaltungsstättenbewilligung model sets capacity and conditions; Bundesliga Stadionbestimmungen require clubs to prove a valid Behördenbescheid. The authority can attach conditions and, under general administrative law for such permits, refuse or revise them. Inspection and technical checks by authorised experts are required under the Stadionbestimmungen and Land practice. Breach can lead to additional conditions, event restrictions, or association sanctions. This is met for the professional football venues in scope; other sports venues depend on Land thresholds.

### Venue infrastructure and operations — met for top-division football

Stadionbestimmungen regulate spectator areas, segregation, buffer zones, pitch protection, access systems, loudspeakers with police priority override, and a control room for the Krisenkontaktgruppe. CCTV with permanent recording covering home and away fan sectors is mandatory for highest-division stadia. Operations manuals appear as Situierungsplan and Ablaufschema for the Ordnerdienst, plus the Sicherheitskonzept under Land law. Outside the Bundesliga, Land instruments and general building/event law apply; this review did not map every Land’s infrastructure detail.

### Emergency and contingency planning — met for evacuation at top football; partial for a universal exercise duty

Bundesliga rules require a authority-approved Fluchtweg- and Evakuierungsplan, including proof of evacuation tests and training. Vienna’s Sicherheitskonzept must include alarm, clearing, and evacuation plans with attention to disabled persons. Contingency for disruptions is covered in club Sicherheitsstrategie and Krisenmanagement duties of the Sicherheitsverantwortlicher and in Ordner duties for bomb threat/evacuation. A single federal statute requiring recorded annual exercises for every designated sports venue was not read; for the highest football division the association rule is clear.

### Venue safety service — met for Ordnungsdienst where Land or Bundesliga rules apply

Styria and Vienna require Ordnungsdienst in the settings described above. Bundesliga Ordnerrichtlinien require Ordner under an Einsatzleiter subordinate to the club Sicherheitsverantwortlicher. Volunteer use in the safety role is not set out as a defined volunteer regime; Ordner must be engaged by employment or written mandate to exercise Hausrecht. That limits informal volunteering in practice but does not spell out a volunteer policy. Partial for volunteers; met for the required service and stewards under the safety officer at top football.

### Access, tickets, and removal from the ground — met for prohibited items, search, and expulsion at top football; partial nationally for disability access and ticketing crime

Bundesliga and ÖFB lists define prohibited items and behaviour, including pyrotechnics and discriminatory banners. Ordner may search at entry (same-sex searches, clothing and bags) and refuse entry or expel under Hausrecht; refusal to surrender a ticket can lead to removal. Ticketing is regulated in association rules (sector assignment, guest cash desks, counterfeit strategy with police). A specific federal criminal offence of unauthorised entry to football matches of the UK 2026 type was not found; general fraud, housebreaking-type offences, and association bans apply. Disability access: Stadionbestimmungen require at least 15 wheelchair places with companion space and an accessible WC, with further recommendations by capacity; Vienna’s concept must address disabled persons in evacuation. A statutory disability access officer post was not found. Federal Behindertengleichstellungsrecht was not re-read in full in this pass.

### Policing, risk, and exclusion — met for NFIP and exclusion tools; partial for statutory risk categories

Police match-day command sits with the security authority’s Einsatzleiter; Bundesliga rules require coordination through the Krisenkontaktgruppe. NFIS is the national football information point. Exclusion: ÖFB Stadionverbotsordnung provides nationwide bans (6 months to 10 years) on a private-law Hausrecht basis, registered and shared with clubs and the BMI. SPG Betretungsverbot excludes from a declared Sicherheitsbereich for the life of that ordinance; Meldeauflage can keep a person away from an event by requiring appearance at a police station. That is not the same as a UK-style court football banning order with passport surrender for foreign matches, but it is a competent-authority exclusion system. A formal statutory scheme that categorises supporters into risk classes with published safeguards was not found; policing uses risk assessment and High-Risk match practice. Database of banned persons: ÖFB/BL list is explicit; the police Gewalttäterdatei Sport is described in secondary sources and is treated here as partial/medium until the RIS paragraphs are confirmed in a later pass.

### Service, dialogue, and inclusion — partial, met for Fanbeauftragter and discrimination rules at top football

Bundesliga clubs must appoint a Fanbeauftragter (supporter liaison). Dialogue with fan clubs is encouraged and, for match-day issues, practised through the Fanbeauftragter and choreography approval rules; a national supporters’ charter or statutory dialogue duty was not found. Discrimination and racist behaviour are banned in house rules, Sicherheitsrichtlinien, and Stadionverbotsordnung, and can be criminal under the Verbotsgesetz and general criminal law (§ 283 StGB). Fan projects or fan embassies as a legal duty were not found. Disability access officer: not found as a named post.

### Sports-event offences — met

Violence, disorder, missile throwing, pitch invasion, pyrotechnics offences, and racist/discriminatory conduct connected with matches are punishable under general criminal law, the Verbotsgesetz, the Pyrotechnikgesetz, SPG administrative offences, and/or association bans. Attacks on players, officials, or Ordner are assault and related offences under ordinary criminal law; a sports-specific aggravation when the victim is a steward was not found. Partial for staff aggravation; met for core conduct.

### Steward status and authorisation — met for top football; partial for a national Annex 2 regime

Ordner are defined in Bundesliga rules as venue safety staff exercising Hausrecht, distinct from the police, under the Sicherheitsverantwortlicher. Fan-sector and pitch-side Ordner need the BL Ordnerschulung and Ordnerausweis, renewed every two years. Commercial Bewachungsgewerbe providers need a Gewerbeberechtigung under GewO 1994 § 129, which expressly covers Ordner- und Kontrolldienste bei Veranstaltungen. Criminal-record vetting as a universal legal precondition for every Ordner was not read as a single federal rule; reliability rules for the Bewachungsgewerbe and club/provider practice apply. There is no separate federal licence to run an in-house stewarding organisation of the Annex 2 model beyond Gewerbe and association rules.

### Steward duties and powers — met for top football

Crowd management, entry control, ticket checks, search, seizure of prohibited items, enforcement of stadium bans, detention under § 80 StPO for judicial offences, and expulsion under Hausrecht are set out in the Ordnerrichtlinien. Powers are expressly grounded in ABGB Hausrecht, StPO, and self-defence — distinct from police powers. Evacuation and bomb-threat tasks are assigned. Match-day roles with police are agreed through the Krisenkontaktgruppe and Sicherheitsbesprechungen.

### Conduct and visibility — partial

Ordner must treat fans politely, de-escalate, and intervene before the police on house-rule breaches and to prevent crime. That is a binding behavioural standard for the highest division, but not a free-standing national code of conduct covering neutrality and non-discrimination by name in the pages read. Recognisable Ordnerwesten are mandatory for Ordner in contact with fans; a unique personal number on the clothing was not required in the text read.

### Records and supervision — partial

Orders, radio traffic, and control-room meetings must be documented. Training must be shown to the Spielbeobachter; failure means removal from fan-sector posts. A required safety handbook issued to every steward covering emergency plans is referred to for pyrotechnics response (BL-Schulungshandbuch) but was not opened as a universal issued handbook duty. Vetting records open to the Land certifying authority were not confirmed as a single rule.

### Training and exercises — partial, stronger for initial training at top football

Initial BL Ordnerschulung before fan-sector/pitch deployment, refreshed every two years, is mandatory. Content detail (crowd safety, first aid, fire, CT, discrimination) was not fully verified from a published curriculum; pyrotechnics response and evacuation duties appear in the Richtlinien. Evacuation tests and training must be evidenced under Stadionbestimmungen. Counter-terrorism awareness and anti-discrimination modules as mandatory syllabus items were not found in the open text.

### Spectator service and private-security law — partial for welcome duties; met for the private-security link

Ordner are required to be polite and welcoming in tone; support for disabled spectators and a formal discrimination-reporting route as steward duties were not read in detail. The private-security link is met: GewO § 129 covers event Ordner- und Kontrolldienste for commercial providers, alongside association rules for club-engaged Ordner.

## What an Austrian reader would be asked

These are the points this review could not close. They are the survey, pre-filled from the findings above.

1. Is there a published national strategy on safety, security, and service (for example the BMI platform “Sicherheit und Sport”), and is it kept under review?
2. Do all nine Länder require a Veranstaltungsstättenbewilligung or equivalent with a stated safe capacity for football stadia above a common threshold, or do the regimes differ materially?
3. Is criminal-record or reliability vetting required by law for every Ordner at designated sports events, including in-house club staff?
4. Does steward training mandatorily cover counter-terrorism awareness and discriminatory behaviour, and where is that syllabus published?
5. Is a unique steward number required on clothing, or only a recognisable Ordnerweste?
6. Is there a named disability access officer (or equivalent) required for Bundesliga or ÖFB events?
7. Please confirm the legal basis and current practice for the Gewalttäterdatei Sport and for sharing ÖFB stadium-ban data with the police.
8. Has any Land or federal instrument adopted or cited Recommendation Rec(2022)2 by name since 2022?
9. What changed in Wiener Veranstaltungsgesetz § 31 from 1 July 2026 that affects football events?
10. Are volunteer Ordner permitted in the safety role, and under what limits?
