# Estonia

Reviewed on 28 September 2026, mainly from official sources in English (Riigi Teataja translations) and Estonian, with Estonian Football Association (EJL) materials. This is the fourth country review, after the [United Kingdom](united-kingdom.md), [Austria](austria.md), and [Belgium](belgium.md). It is a desk review: each topic from [the codebook](../codebook/codebook.md) was looked for in the Sport Act, the Security Activities Act, Police and Border Guard / NFIP references, municipal permit practice, and EJL stadium and house rules. Recommendation Rec(2022)2 was also searched for by name.

Estonia is a State Party to the Saint-Denis Convention. It signed on 14 October 2019, ratified on 16 December 2019, and the Convention entered into force for Estonia on 1 February 2020. That is before Rec(2022)2 was adopted. The Convention text is published in Riigi Teataja (RT II, 16.01.2020, 1). No source checked in this review cites Recommendation Rec(2022)2 by name.

## The short answer

Estonia requires a **municipal permit** for designated sports events, including national adult football-league games, UEFA club cup matches, national-team matches, and events with an increased security risk. The Sport Act places clear organiser duties: a named person responsible for public order and security, either a licensed security undertaking with a security plan or organiser-provided stewards with described duties, supporter segregation, ticket-sale information, parking, and a security-risk assessment. The Police and Border Guard Board and the Rescue Board can approve (including conditionally). That covers a large mid-slice of Annex 1 for football events in scope.

It has **not** adopted a Belgium-style dedicated Football Act or a dedicated national stewarding regulation of Annex 2. Stewarding sits either under the **Security Activities Act** (licensed turvaettevõtja / security guards) or as organiser “korrapidajad” (stewards) whose number and duties are declared in the permit file — without the full Annex 2 package of Interior-approved training, unique numbering, and handbook duties found in Belgium. Stadium infrastructure detail for top football is driven heavily by **EJL stadium category standards** and venue house rules (e.g. A. Le Coq Arena), not by a separate federal stadium-safety certificate agency.

Administrative / judicial football banning orders of the UK or Belgian type were **not found** as a statutory scheme. Exclusion is mainly civil (house rules / EJL disciplinary) plus ordinary public-order and criminal law. An NFIP function in the Police and Border Guard Board is documented in EU NFIP contact lists.

## What has changed since 1 September 2022

- **1 July 2024.** Security Activities Act reform links (RT I, 01.03.2023, 2) take effect in the Sport Act permit line: security undertaking + security plan under the security-activities policy, **or** organiser stewards and their duties.
- **1 December 2024.** Motorsport liability-insurance rules under the Sport Act (related amendment).
- **10 July 2025 / staged 2026 force.** Spordiseaduse muutmise seadus (RT I, 10.07.2025, 1) revises sports-event organisation: clarifies which events need a permit; adds **accessibility / disability-inclusion planning** and **organiser pyrotechnics information** to the permit application (as published in the amending text); introduces / refines **season permits**; strengthens organiser pyrotechnics duties; raises the fine for legal persons for organisation breaches (reported towards €20,000). Several provisions enter into force on **1 January 2026** and **1 June 2026**. This review treats those as **amended / new** where the text is already published; in-force dates are noted.
- **2025.** EJL *Staadionite standard* category document for stadium infrastructure (version read for this pass).

No Estonian instrument checked cites Rec(2022)2.

## How the framework is organised

**Sport Act (Spordiseadus).** The core public statute for event organisation. Designated events need a rural-municipality or city-government permit (§ 20). Applications must name the person responsible for public order and security, set out security company/plan or steward numbers and duties, segregation of supporters, ticketing, parking, and a risk assessment. Permit grant/refusal and conditions sit with the local authority; Police and Border Guard Board and Rescue Board approve. Organiser obligations include participant/spectator security, segregation, public order, and compliance with approval conditions (§ 23). Misdemeanour liability for organising without a permit or breaching organiser duties (§ 25).

**Security Activities Act (Turvaseadus).** Licenses security undertakings and regulates security staff who maintain order at events. Where the organiser uses a security company, that regime supplies qualification, plan, and powers. It is a **private-security** regime, not a football-specific Annex 2 stewarding code.

**Convention in domestic law.** RT II, 16.01.2020, 1 publishes the Saint-Denis Convention. That supports the integrated approach internationally for Estonia but does not by itself invent a full domestic Annex 1 statute.

**EJL / venues.** Stadium category standards, disciplinary rules, and A. Le Coq Arena house rules cover CCTV practice, prohibited items, searches by security staff, refusal of entry, discrimination bans, and evacuation instructions at the main national venue. These are binding for association/venue purposes where they apply; they are not a substitute for a national stewarding regulation.

**NFIP.** EU Council NFIP contact lists place Estonia’s NFIP in the Police and Border Guard Board (Public Order Police Department / Coordination Division), with the historic contact nfip.estonia@list.pol.ee. This review did not open a fresh PPA organisational page confirming the exact unit name in 2026.

## Topic by topic

### National governance and coordination — partial

No single statutory national committee whose job is safety, security and service together (Annex 1 style) was found. Coordination for events runs through municipal permits and Police/Rescue approval. The NFIP covers the police information pillar. Local multi-agency coordination is **met** in substance for permitted events (municipality + Police + Rescue + organiser). A published national strategy on safety, security and service kept under formal review was **not found**. Roles of organiser, local authority, police, and (where used) security company are stated across the Sport Act and Security Activities Act — **met** for events in scope.

### Venue safety duties — met for organiser and responsible person; partial for integrated statutory duty and regulations

Organiser safety/public-order duties and a named responsible person are required for permitted events. Venue regulations as a condition of entry are clear at A. Le Coq Arena and typical for ticketed football; a universal statutory duty that every designated football venue must publish house rules was not separately opened beyond permit/association practice — treated as **met** for top venues in practice, with a survey note. An integrated safety–security–service duty is the language of the Convention (in force) and of the Sport Act’s multi-agency permit; it is not a single Football Act inventing the three pillars for all sports. **Partial** for a universal integrated statute; **met** for operator and safety-officer-equivalent (responsible person) on permitted football events.

### Safety certification and licensing — partial / met for event permit, not for a stadium safety certificate model

The municipal sports-event **permit** is required before designated football events. The authority can refuse or attach conditions; Police/Rescue approval feeds that. That is **met** for the event-licence idea. It is **not** the same as a standing stadium safety certificate with a fixed safe capacity issued by a specialist agency (UK SGSA / Belgian mayor attestation + norms model). Safe capacity appears via venue/EJL category and permit conditions rather than a dedicated capacity certificate statute — **partial**. Inspection and enforcement exist through approval agencies and misdemeanour fines — **met** at medium confidence for enforcement; inspection of stadium fabric is less clearly a periodic Sport Act duty — **partial**.

### Venue infrastructure and operations — partial nationally; stronger under EJL standards

EJL stadium standards regulate access, turnstiles (for higher categories), sector marking, evacuation doors, emergency lighting, control room for police/security, etc. Sport Act requires segregation information and parking organisation in the permit. CCTV is present and disclosed at A. Le Coq Arena; a statutory nationwide CCTV duty for all designated football stadia was **not found**. Operational arrangements with police sit in the approval/permit process. Overall **partial** nationally, **met** for top-category EJL venues on infrastructure/segregation where the standard applies.

### Emergency and contingency planning — partial

Permit risk assessment and security plans (when a security undertaking is used) cover risks and order maintenance. A. Le Coq rules require compliance with evacuation orders. A statutory universal emergency plan + recorded exercise cycle of the Belgian art. 10 type was **not found**. **Partial**.

### Venue safety service — met for required service option; partial for stewards-under-safety-officer and volunteers

The law requires either a security undertaking/plan or organiser stewards with described duties. That **meets** “a safety service is required” for permitted events. Chain of command under the named responsible person is implied; Annex 2-style steward hierarchy is not spelled out. Volunteer steward limits are not set out as a dedicated volunteer regime — **partial**.

### Access, tickets, and removal — met for search/prohibited items/expulsion at main venues; partial for disability and ticketing crime

House rules and EJL practice: searches, prohibited items (weapons, pyrotechnics, alcohol, etc.), refuse entry / eject. Ticketing must be described in the permit; electronic ticketing for higher EJL categories. No UK-style unauthorised-entry football offence found. Disability: the July 2025 amending Act adds accessibility/inclusion information to the permit application (force dates in 2026) — **partial → strengthening**; not yet read as a full access-standard duty. Discrimination is banned in A. Le Coq rules.

### Policing, risk, and exclusion — met for police role and NFIP; partial for risk categories; not found / partial for statutory bans and database

Police approve permits and keep public order; match policing practice uses risk assessment. NFIP **met**. Statutory supporter risk-category scheme with safeguards **not found** (partial for operational risk assessment). Administrative/judicial football banning orders and a central banned-persons database **not found** as Sport Act instruments; civil exclusion and EJL discipline apply — **partial** for bans, **not found** for a national ban database.

### Service, dialogue, and inclusion — partial / not found for most dialogue duties

No statutory SLO or DAO duty found. Discrimination addressed in venue rules and general equality/criminal law. Fan projects as a legal duty **not found**. Dialogue strategy **not found**. Survey questions apply.

### Sports-event offences — met via general law; partial for staff aggravation

Disorder, violence, pitch invasion, pyrotechnics misuse are punishable under general criminal / misdemeanour law and association rules; Sport Act punishes organisation breaches. Sports-staff-specific aggravation when the victim is a steward **not found** — **partial**.

### Steward status and Annex 2 cluster — partial overall; stronger where Security Activities Act applies

Stewards/security staff are distinct from police. Qualification/vetting/provider authorisation are **met** for licensed security guards under the Security Activities Act; **partial / not found** for organiser-only stewards on Annex 2 training, CT modules, unique visible numbers, issued handbooks, and welcome/discrimination-reporting duties. Fluorescent identification and codes of conduct were not found as national football-steward rules. Private-security link is **met** (dedicated security law + Sport Act option to use it).

## What an Estonian reader would be asked

1. Is there a published national strategy on safety, security and service at football / sports events, and is it kept under review?
2. Does any authority issue a standing stadium safety certificate with a stated safe capacity (beyond the municipal event permit and EJL category standards)?
3. Are organiser-provided stewards (*korrapidajad*) subject to mandatory training, vetting, and identification rules equivalent to licensed security guards?
4. Is there a statutory administrative or judicial football stadium ban, and a central database used at ticket gates?
5. Are Supporter Liaison Officers and Disability Access Officers required by law or only by UEFA/EJL licensing practice?
6. Is CCTV mandatory at all designated football stadia, or only where EJL category / venue rules require it?
7. What is the current organisational home and public page of the Estonian NFIP within the Police and Border Guard Board?
8. Has any Estonian instrument adopted or cited Recommendation Rec(2022)2 by name since 2022?
9. Which July 2025 Sport Act amendments are already in force for football matches as of this review, and which wait until 2026?
10. Are emergency plans and recorded evacuation exercises required by statute for all permitted football events?
